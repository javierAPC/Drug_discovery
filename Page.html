Methodology 01

Pipeline Overview

1
Data Collection & Cleaning
ChEMBL API query (CHEMBL203, IC50, binding assays). Quality filters: pChEMBL not null, no duplicates, exact measurements only. InChI key deduplication with variable-measurement averaging.
→ 9,876 unique compounds
2
Exploratory Analysis
Lipinski Ro5 profile, pIC50 distribution, chemical space visualisation (MW vs LogP). Mann-Whitney U tests for descriptor significance. FDA drugs marked as reference.
→ 6 EDA figures
3
Molecular Featurization
Three complementary representations: ECFP6 (339 bits), MACCS keys (1115), RDKit 2D descriptors (164). VarianceThreshold for binary bits; Pearson correlation filter for continuous descriptors.
→ 3 feature matrices saved
4
Butina CV Baseline
Butina clustering at Tanimoto sim_cutoff=0.6 using Morgan fingerprint. 5-fold CV with cluster integrity — test sets contain complete structural clusters never seen in training. RF ablation study across all three feature sets.
→ Scaffold-aware split established
5
Model Comparison & Tuning
Optuna TPE, 50 trials per model (RF, XGBoost, SVR). Inner 3-fold on fold-1 training data prevents leakage. PyTorch Lightning MLP (3-layer, BatchNorm, Dropout) as DL comparison.
→ XGBoost best: R² = 0.727
6
SHAP Explainability
TreeExplainer on MACCS-retrained model. MACCS keys chosen over ECFP6 because each bit has a fixed SMARTS definition — directly interpretable without lookup tables.
→ Pharmacophore alignment confirmed
7
Applicability Domain
Williams plot (leverage) and Tanimoto distance (threshold 0.4). UMAP of full chemical space with FDA drugs marked. Virtual screen of EGFR Ki compounds not in training set.
→ AD-flagged screen hits
8
Deployment
Streamlit prediction app: input any SMILES → predicted pChEMBL + IC50 equivalent + activity class + Lipinski radar + Tanimoto AD check + structural similarity to FDA drugs.
→ Docker · Huggingface

XGBoost and SVR achieve identical mean R² (0.724). XGBoost was selected as the final model for three technical reasons: lower variance across folds (0.008 vs 0.011), faster SHAP explainability, and better scalability with large datasets compared to SVR. The gap relative to Vignaux et al. (0.724 vs 0.810) is explained by the stricter Butina split — random cross‑validation allows near‑duplicate compounds in both training and test sets, inflating R² by an estimated 15–30% (Wu et al., 2018, MoleculeNet). Different targets (EGFR vs AChE) also limit direct comparability.

Explainability 03

SHAP Analysis — MACCS Keys

TreeExplainer (exact Shapley values) applied to XGBoost retrained on MACCS features. The model captures chemically meaningful SAR, with both favorable and unfavorable motifs emerging in patterns consistent with EGFR binding mechanisms.

Hinge region — Met793
H-bond donor motifs
MACCS 131 (H-bond donors, OH/NH count >1) · MACCS 84 (NH₂ group) · MACCS 53 (two heteroatoms with H separated by 4 atoms)

Mixed SHAP distribution: presence often increases activity but with a significant negative tail, indicating context-dependent effects rather than uniformly beneficial contributions.
Aromatic stacking & scaffolding
Ring systems
MACCS 98 (6-membered heterocycles) · MACCS 101 (aromatic amine ArNH₂) · MACCS 62 (ring assembly) · MACCS 115 (ring)

Aromatic features are generally beneficial, but contributions vary: heterocycles show strong positive effects, while other ring motifs act more as structural enablers.
Covalent warhead — Cys797
Alkene motifs
MACCS 34 (CH₂=CH– alkene motif)

Absence strongly decreases activity, while presence yields only moderate gains, suggesting electrophilic motifs are important but not dominant across all compounds.
Linker geometry & penalties
Spacing & motifs
MACCS 138 (heteroatom–CH₂– group count >1) · MACCS 52 (NN motif) · MACCS 38 (NC(C)N)

Linker-related features define spatial arrangement: some improve activity, while others act as anti-pharmacophoric motifs, penalizing predictions.
SHAP patterns show that the model encodes both positive drivers (e.g. MACCS 98, 138) and penalizing features (e.g. MACCS 38, 52), with several motifs exhibiting context-dependent behavior. This indicates the model captures realistic structure–activity trade-offs rather than simple correlations.

Caveats 05

Limitations

L1
Single assay type. Trained on IC50 binding assay data only. Predictions for cell-based (functional) assays or different measurement types (Ki, EC50) involve assay-type assumptions that are not validated here.
L2
Wild-type EGFR only. T790M and L858R resistance mutations are not modelled. These require separate datasets (CHEMBL3736, CHEMBL3214805). Osimertinib's activity against T790M is not within the model's applicability domain.
L3
2D features only. ECFP6 and MACCS encode molecular topology without 3D geometry. Stereoisomers with the same graph but different spatial arrangements may receive identical predictions.
L4
AD is approximate. Both leverage (h*) and Tanimoto thresholds are heuristics. The optimal threshold is dataset-dependent and not calibrated here against experimental hit rates.
L5
No experimental validation. Virtual screen hits are computational predictions only. Wet-lab confirmation (biochemical IC50 assay) is required before drawing biological conclusions.
L6
Bias towards active class. Despite the use of methods to address class imbalance, the model presents a bias towards the active class; to be more precise, it tends to overestimate the pChEMBL of the targets.
